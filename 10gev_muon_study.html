10 GeV vertical-muon response of the MuonSLab plastic-scintillator stack, from a 1,000-muon run (2026-07-22). Each event is a 10 GeV μ⁻ fired through the slab centre, straight down through the four layers. The notebook reports the energy loss per layer and the photoelectron yield of every SiPM channel — means ± RMS over the 1,000 muons, plus the per-channel NPE distributions. Geant4 11.4.2 + ROOT.

Note: the SiPMs are modelled at 10 × 10 mm (1 cm²), larger than a real 3×3 / 6×6 mm device, so the photoelectron numbers below are inflated accordingly (details at the end).

1. Setup & geometry¶

Four slabs of EJ-200-type plastic (10 000 γ/MeV, Birks k = 0.126 mm/MeV, n = 1.58), each 20 × 20 × 2 cm, wrapped in ESR + tape and read out by 12 SiPMs per slab (three on each ±X / ±Y face, 48 channels total), air-coupled. Three slabs cluster at z ≈ 42 / 64 / 86 mm; the fourth sits at z ≈ −118 mm below an aluminium battery box.

2. Energy loss per layer¶

Every muon crosses all four layers. The mean dE/dx sits above the most-probable value because of the Landau high-energy-loss tail (thin-slab regime). Layer 4 — the slab below the aluminium battery box — has a higher mean and larger RMS from delta-ray / shower contamination produced in the ~13 cm of aluminium above it.

3. Light yield per SiPM¶

Each muon produces ~130 k scintillation photons; ~236 are detected as photoelectrons (≈ 13.8 p.e./MeV), spread over the 48 channels. The response is close to uniform across channels, as expected for a muon through the slab centre.

4. NPE distribution per SiPM (all 48 channels)¶

Each panel is the photoelectron-count distribution of one SiPM over the 1,000 muons (integer NPE; red dashed line = mean). Rows are layers (top → bottom), columns are the 12 SiPM face-positions. The distributions are Poisson-like, peaking around ~5 p.e. and nearly identical across channels, confirming the uniform response to a centred muon.

5. Summary¶

Energy loss is ~4.0–4.6 MeV per 2 cm layer (mean dE/dx ≈ 2.1 MeV/cm, the Landau tail above the MIP-most-probable ~1.6 MeV/cm), totalling 17.1 ± 4.0 MeV over the four layers; layer 4 runs high (4.6 ± 2.6 MeV) from delta-ray contamination out of the aluminium battery box. Light yield is ~236 photoelectrons per muon (≈ 13.8 p.e./MeV), distributed almost uniformly over the 48 SiPMs at about 5 p.e. per channel, with a narrow, Poisson-like per-channel spread. Every channel sees a clean, near-identical NPE spectrum, so the detector response to a centred MIP is uniform and well-behaved.

6. Caveats & reproduce¶

The SiPM active area is 10 × 10 mm in this model (config.yaml SiPM.x_length = y_length = 1.0 cm), not a realistic device size — re-run with 3×3 or 6×6 mm before quoting absolute photoelectron yields, since they scale with active area. The SiPMs are also air-coupled (no optical grease), giving a low ~0.14 % light collection. The template was ported from Geant4 10.6 to 11.4; the scintillation yield was verified (G4-11 renamed FASTCOMPONENT → SCINTILLATIONCOMPONENT1, which had silently produced zero scintillation light before the fix).

The 1,000 muons were run as four parallel chunks (out_c0..c3.root); this notebook aggregates all four.
